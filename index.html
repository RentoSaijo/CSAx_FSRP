Reading the score

CSAx compares a player’s recorded contact and puck-battle behavior with the physical profile expected from their listed size. Higher values indicate more physical presence relative to size within the same position and season. The score describes a pattern of play; it does not measure courage, skill, or overall player value.

Who appears here?

351 forwards and 205 defensemen with at least 500 total regular-season NHL minutes and 150 matched five-on-five tracked minutes in 2024–25.

Technical details

The calculations below describe the fitted 2024–25 scores exactly as a procedure. Coefficients and transformations are learned separately for each position, season, and assessment fold, so there is no single universal list of numerical weights.

1. Listed size

Within each position-season reference population, standardize listed height H and weight W. Form their equal-weight combination, then standardize it again:

zH,i = (Hi − μH) / sH zW,i = (Wi − μW) / sW Ci = (zH,i + zW,i) / √2 Si = (Ci − μC) / sC

Means and sample standard deviations come from all eligible native player-seasons in that position and season. S is the listed-size outcome; it is not the CSAx score.

2. Behavior-predicted size and frame calibration

For each position and season, a ridge model predicts S from direct contact and position-specific indirect features. For transformed predictors z, its fitted prediction and objective are:

xSi = β0,−k + Σj βj,−kzij,−k min [Σi(Si − β0 − Σjβjzij)² / (2n) + λΣjβj² / 2]

Five outer player-grouped folds give each player-season one prediction from a model that excluded that player. Within each outer training set, five inner player-grouped folds choose the ridge penalty by mean squared error from 20 values spaced logarithmically between 0.0001 and 100.

Training samples supply median imputation, removal of constant features, Yeo–Johnson transformations, and predictor standardization. Let xSi be the outer-held-out ridge prediction. A linear calibration learned from inner held-out predictions in the outer training set gives the prediction normally associated with listed size Si:

m−k(Si) = a−k + b−kSi ri = xSi − m−k(Si)

The calibration prevents ordinary regression shrinkage from mechanically favoring smaller players. It uses no behavior from the outer assessment player.

3. CSAx and percentile

Center and standardize the held-out residuals within the native position-season reference. The percentile uses midranks, including half the players tied at the same residual:

CSAxi = (ri − r̄ref) / sr,ref Percentilei = 100 × [#{rj < ri} + ½ #{rj = ri}] / Nref

A score of +1 is one reference residual standard deviation above that position-season mean. The forward and defenseman scores have separate reference scales.

4. Features and denominators

All count rates equal 60 × count / matched five-on-five minutes. The six direct inputs in both positional models are hits delivered, hits received, opponent shots blocked, fights, contact penalties taken, and contact penalties drawn. Contact penalties count recorded infractions from a predefined contact-related whitelist; fighting is counted separately.

Forwards

  • Net-front attempt share: located unblocked attempts from 82 to 89 feet toward the attacking net and within eight feet of center ice, divided by all located unblocked attempts.
  • Tip/deflection share: typed tip or deflection shots divided by typed shots on goal, including goals.
  • Median distance from the net for located unblocked attempts, in feet.
  • Backhand share: typed backhand shots divided by typed shots on goal, including goals.
  • All Three Zones dump-in recoveries and forecheck pressures, each per 60 matched minutes.

Defensemen

  • All Three Zones clean defensive-zone retrievals and botched retrievals, each per 60 matched minutes.
  • Entry denial share: recorded denials divided by targeted entries.

Undefined shares remain missing until training-sample imputation. The analysis requires at least 300 full-season NHL minutes and 150 matched tracked minutes; publication here additionally requires 500 full-season minutes. Recorded games are selected, not a complete census of every shift.

Sources and interpretation

We combine NHL game and shift records with Corey Sznajder’s All Three Zones transition workbook. See the A3Z glossary for tracked-event definitions. CSAx describes a learned physical profile, not the success of every individual play. Uneven tracking, differences in player roles, and the weak 2024–25 defenseman size prediction limit interpretation. This is independent research; no NHL or All Three Zones endorsement is implied.
